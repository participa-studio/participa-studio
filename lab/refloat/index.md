challenge: 'Images of old boat wrecks carry a certain melancholy, solitude, a sense of history. As beautiful as they are here, the reality behind them is far from romantic. In the Netherlands alone, an estimated 2,500 leisure boats need scrapping every year, and around 25,000 lie abandoned. Unlike France, the country has no scheme that pays for their end of life, so the cost falls on the last owner, or on no one. I saw this first-hand and reflected on it with fellow sailors and peers from other initiatives (e.g. <a href="https://www.floatingstorieslab.org/regenerative-tides" title="Floating Stories Lab">FSL</a>). From there, I started ReFloat as an iteration of my <a href="/lab/escola-da-costa/">Escola da Costa</a> project. ReFloat’s mission: care for the local coast, teach what the ocean receives, act on what boats leave behind, and measure the impact.'
approach: 'The work grows in steps. It starts by scouting possible locations: the communities along the coast, the local government, the boats themselves and nearby schools. A first pilot tests the thinking with them; a first local workshop is now being explored by the Sloterplas in Amsterdam, and Portugal and Brazil are also under study. What the pilot confirms becomes the business case for a first location, and the legal form follows from what that place needs. Once a site is in place, every object moves through five stages: raise awareness, collect, repair, transform, recycle; a boat that can still sail is kept sailing first. Four pillars carry the work: dismantling as an education platform, reuse and repurpose, learning through work for trainees far from the labour market, and responsible disposal with a certificate for the owner. On the R-ladder of circular strategies, the work sits on repair, refurbish and repurpose.'
outcome: 'ReFloat is a work in progress. On 10 September 2026, after a conversation with fellow sailors at the Floating Stories Lab booth in Lorient, I drafted the first concept and business plan with the <a href="/lab/social-enterprise-canvas/">Social Enterprise Canvas</a>; on 2 November 2026 I published the concept openly on <a href="https://refloat.earth/">refloat.earth</a>. It exists as a first iteration of a business plan. The next steps test it on the ground, with interviews around a first location, so that a solid, tested business plan is in place by July 2027. Milestones on the way: conversations with a host workshop and a pilot marina, a first awareness session open to neighbours, a theory of change before any metric, and a go or no-go decision on location and legal form. Income is expected from the shop, sessions for schools and organisations, and fees for boat removal; how it is shared is still to be decided. First measures to test: kilograms kept from waste, parts reused, products made, trainees placed, certified disposals, pupils reached and cases published. The aim is for ReFloat to be governed with its community.'
