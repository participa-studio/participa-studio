challenge: 'What happens to pleasure boats at the end of their life? ReFloat started with that question, in a conversation among sailors on 10 September 2026. Its mission: care for the local coast, teach what the ocean receives, act on what boats leave behind, and measure the impact.'
approach: 'Every object moves through five stages: raise awareness, collect, repair, transform, recycle; a boat that can still sail is kept sailing first. Four pillars carry the work: dismantling as an education platform, reuse and repurpose, learning through work for trainees far from the labour market, and responsible disposal with a certificate for the owner. On the R-ladder of circular strategies, the work sits on repair, refurbish and repurpose.'
outcome: 'The first concept and business plan were drafted with the Social Enterprise Canvas. Income is to come from the shop, sessions for schools and organisations, and fees for boat removal; learners often do not pay. Measurement starts from zero: kilograms kept from waste, parts reused, products made, trainees placed, boats disposed of with a certificate, pupils, and published cases. ReFloat is to be governed with its community; its legal form is not yet decided.'
