challenge: 'The trigger was a visit in person to Thiago Máximo, co-founder of the Institut Supérieur de Design de Saint-Malo, on the French coast. It is a cooperative, owned with its town and partners, and students learn on real assignments from local businesses and government. It showed me that design education can take a completely different form. Reflecting on it with Felipe Schmidt Fonseca, I asked him plainly: what would it take to have a school like that on the Brazilian coast? Ubatuba sits between the Atlantic Forest and the ocean, with no local higher education; young people leave and rarely return. And concern for the ocean is not understanding: in a 2022 survey of 18 to 24 year-olds in 35 countries, 75% were worried about ocean health, yet 47% believed it was still healthy.'
approach: 'An application to <a href="https://www.designscience.studio">Design Science Studio</a>, a programme grounded in Buckminster Fuller''s design science, gave me the reason to pick up an early concept and research it properly. I documented it, produced videos to present it to the group, and wrote a proposal for the Artist Salon in April 2026. The idea matured into an education platform tied to the ocean: a cooperative school where fishing, caiçara and quilombola communities hold the decisions, and learning, livelihood and care for the coast are one thing. Design Science Studio has a strong drive towards personal and performative arts; I was looking for someone to talk with about building a social enterprise. It was not the right forum to develop the idea, but it did mature the concept. Preparing for the MARRES programme, which I did not apply to in the end, pushed me to ground the concept in marine social science: the aim became to investigate and work with the interaction between the ocean, its communities and nature. In November 2026 it became a first business plan.'
outcome: 'None of it was built: no cooperative, no site, no funding. Looking back, the concept drifted with every door it was written for. It started as a school in the hands of the community and ended as a business plan priced in euros for international students, close to the educational tourism it set out to avoid. That is why it was parked. What survives is real: its principles became the Participa Principles, and its education-first frame shaped <a href="/lab/refloat/">ReFloat</a>. The lesson I keep: a school starts with the people who will run it, not with a plan written for funders.'
