challenge: 'For more than a year I had been looking for enterprises that put design to work for social and regenerative purposes. Conversations with my friend Felipe Schmidt Fonseca led me to the <a href="https://institut.design">Institut Supérieur de Design de Saint-Malo</a>, on the French coast. It is a cooperative, owned with its town and partners, and students learn on real assignments from local businesses and government. It showed me that design education can take a completely different form, and a visit with its co-founder, Thiago Máximo, confirmed it. I asked plainly: what would it take to have a school like that on the Brazilian coast? Ubatuba became one candidate location, through Felipe, who lives there; I also looked at Rio Grande do Norte, in connection with a university group. Ubatuba sits between the Atlantic Forest and the ocean, with no local higher education; young people leave and rarely return. And caring about the ocean is not the same as understanding it: in a 2024 survey of 18 to 24 year-olds in 35 countries, 75% were worried about ocean health, yet 47% believed it was still healthy.'
approach: 'The approach was exploratory: each step tested the concept from a different side.</p><p><strong>Form.</strong> In Saint-Malo, with Thiago and Cesar Harada, founder of MakerBay in Hong Kong, I talked through how such a school could be organised. My first version was a structured organisation with its own site; Felipe argued for a decentralised platform closer to a Fab Lab. Neither convinced me.</p><p><strong>People.</strong> In IDEO U’s <a href="https://www.ideou.com/products/designing-for-change">Designing for Change</a> course, completed in November 2025, I mapped the groups the school would serve and prepared interview questions for specialists and community leaders.</p><p><strong>Presentation.</strong> For <a href="https://www.designscience.studio">Design Science Studio</a> (BFI), I turned the concept into a business plan: a cooperative on the French SCIC model, summer schools whose fees would fund places for local youth, and a phased start in existing local spaces. It matured the concept, but the programme was not the forum to develop it.</p><p><strong>Grounding.</strong> A call in November 2025 with <a href="https://univ-cotedazur.eu/msc/marres">MARRES</a>, the Blue Managers track at Université Côte d’Azur, and reading in marine social science, social design and anthropology anchored the concept in life on the coast.'
outcome: 'None of it was built: no cooperative, no site, no funding. The more the concept grew, the less tangible it became, and I could not formalise it. What carried over is the frame: education as the point, not the product. <a href="/lab/refloat/">ReFloat</a> started as the idea of a ressourcerie, the French model of a local centre that collects, repairs and resells what others throw away: here, taking end-of-life boats apart for reuse and recycling, as many small enterprises already do. It gained weight when I brought in the educational side of Escola da Costa: a ressourcerie raises awareness, but the ReFloat makerspace would become a school, and the boat would become a way into a community conversation about the ocean, its nature and its role in the community. In that sense, ReFloat builds on where Escola da Costa stopped.'
