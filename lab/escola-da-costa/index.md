challenge: 'The trigger was a visit in person to Thiago Máximo, co-founder of the <a href="https://institut.design">Institut Supérieur de Design de Saint-Malo</a>, on the French coast. It is a cooperative, owned with its town and partners, and students learn on real assignments from local businesses and government. It showed me that design education can take a completely different form. Reflecting on it with Felipe Schmidt Fonseca, I asked him plainly: what would it take to have a school like that on the Brazilian coast? Ubatuba sits between the Atlantic Forest and the ocean, with no local higher education; young people leave and rarely return. And concern for the ocean is not understanding: in a 2022 survey of 18 to 24 year-olds in 35 countries, 75% were worried about ocean health, yet 47% believed it was still healthy.'
approach: 'During the same visit to Saint-Malo, I spent the day with Thiago and had dinner with a Fab Lab founder from Saint-Malo who now lives in Asia. We talked, informally, about how such an enterprise could be organised. The concept first took shape as my project in IDEO U''s Designing for Change course, completed in November 2025. There I mapped the groups the school would serve and prepared interview questions for Felipe and for a marine researcher. The harder conversations were about form. My first version leaned towards a structured organisation with its own location. Felipe resisted a fixed, established place and leaned towards a decentralised education platform, closer to a Fab Lab. Neither model convinced me as a future business. An application to <a href="https://www.designscience.studio">Design Science Studio</a>, a programme grounded in Buckminster Fuller''s design science, gave me the reason to research the concept properly. I documented it, produced videos to present it to the group, and wrote a proposal for the Artist Salon. The programme leaned towards personal and performative arts, and I was looking for someone to talk with about building a social enterprise. It matured the concept, but it was not the forum to develop it. In the same period it became a first business plan. Preparing for the MARRES programme, which I did not apply to in the end, grounded the concept in marine social science: social design and anthropology applied to life on the coast. The aim became a continuous conversation between a community, the ocean in front of it, its fauna and flora, and the materials at hand. The work on the coast would improve the ocean and the lives that depend on it.'
outcome: 'None of it was built: no cooperative, no site, no funding. The more the concept grew, the less tangible it became, and I could not formalise it. It is parked. What carried over is the frame. <a href="/lab/refloat/">ReFloat</a> started as taking end-of-life boats apart for recycling, which many small enterprises already do. It took shape when education became the point: the workshop as a school, and the boat as the way into a community conversation about the ocean and its nature. In that sense, ReFloat is Escola da Costa 2.0. Its thinking also shaped the principles behind Participa.'
