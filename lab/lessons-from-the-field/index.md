challenge: 'In the Netherlands a place with a social purpose has to choose a legal form: a stichting (foundation), a vereniging (association), a coöperatie (cooperative) or a not-for-profit company. Each form decides who votes, who owns and what happens to a surplus, and that choice shapes how open the place can stay. The business-model literature says little about this. It does not ask where the rules should live, where informal hierarchy sits in a flat structure, or what these places mean by “good”. The question came up while choosing a legal form for <a href="/lab/refloat/">ReFloat</a>, and it is wider than one project.'
approach: 'The main question: how do beyond-profit places in the Netherlands organise participation and collaboration so that they stay open, keep deciding together and sustain themselves? Ten questions follow from it, on how a place starts, how open it is, where its rules live, how it decides, how it cares for shared tools, where hierarchy sits, how money comes in, what its legal form prevents, how it grows and what it counts as good. The method combines visits and volunteering, public documents and founder interviews of about an hour, in Dutch where useful. Each interview adds a column to a comparison table and a row checked against Elinor Ostrom’s eight principles for governing a commons. Participa adds a ninth: the place has standing, so the interview also asks who speaks for the place and what they can stop. The first places studied are Buurtwerkplaats Noorderhof, De Sering, Post Office, the Dutch kringloop (second-hand) sector and BlueCity in Rotterdam, with La Recyclerie Maritime in France as an outside reference.'
outcome: 'Lessons from the field is a work in progress. I opened the research on 3 October 2026 with the research questions, the interview guide and a first comparison of four places drawn from public sources. I visited BlueCity in November 2026, and the first founder interviews are being arranged. A first reading, still to be tested in the interviews: the place that writes its rules as a commons meets all eight of Ostrom’s principles, while the place that is open to anyone struggles most with looking after shared tools. The findings feed the legal-form decision and the social canvas of ReFloat, and the Participa principles. How the results are published is still open: a living comparison table, a series of interviews or a short summary.'
