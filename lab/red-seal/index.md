challenge: 'I had wanted my own sailing boat for over five years and visited boats for sale in Spain, Portugal and the Netherlands to learn what I wanted. An eight-day Arctic expedition to Svalbard in 2023 made my criteria clear: steel, suitable for short-handed sailing and homely below deck. The Van de Stadt Zeehond 36 went to the top of my list. Even with no boat in sight, as a designer I could not help giving shape to that sense of ownership. From August 2025, I wrote narratives, rated names and drafted a myth connecting my Brazilian roots with my Dutch history.'
approach: 'I compared names on VHF clarity, international readability, heritage and personal meaning, and chose BearWolf: a polar bear for the north and a maned wolf (lobo-guará) for the south. I designed a crest and boat renderings around it. When I visited the boat I later bought, in February 2026, her own name took over. She was launched as SEAL, which is also what her design model''s name, Zeehond, means in Dutch. I kept the north–south idea, but the polar bear made way for a seal, and the maned wolf stayed for the south. SEAL needed an edge to make it distinct, so I added Red, taken from the wolf side of the myth: the guará and urucum, the red pigment from Brazil. The boat became Red Seal.'
outcome: 'In May 2026, I redesigned the crest: a wolf profile with a seal head cut out of it, above three waves. I tested it against the bear-and-wolf version and the new mark held. I built the palette around wine red and sun yellow. The myth, <em>Between the Bear and the Wolf</em>, kept the original pair and already carried the name Red Seal; I recorded it in English and Portuguese. The crest is now on her burgees, beanies, document binders, doormat and engraved Van de Stadt design plate. The bow crest and hull lettering will follow at the April 2027 haul-out. Thanks to her builder, Hermanus van Middelkoop, and to her former owner, Holger Fuchs, who kept her and all her documents before passing her on to me.'
