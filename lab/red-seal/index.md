challenge: 'I had wanted my own sailing boat for over five years. I visited boats for sale at marinas in Spain, Portugal, and around the Netherlands to see them up close and learn what I wanted: layout, headroom, engine access, and so on. Only after an eight-day expedition to the Arctic did my criteria become clear: steel, suitable for short-handed sailing, and homely below deck. With future Arctic trips in mind, the Van de Stadt Zeehond 36 went to the top of my list. Even before I had any concrete prospect of owning a boat, as a designer I could not help creating artefacts that gave shape to that sense of ownership. Beginning in August 2025, I wrote narratives, rated every name I found interesting, and drafted several versions of a myth connecting my Brazilian roots with my Dutch history. I compared name candidates on VHF clarity, international readability, heritage, and personal meaning, eventually choosing BearWolf: a polar bear for the north and a maned wolf (lobo-guará) for the south. I designed a crest and boat renderings for it. Yet when I visited the boat I later bought in February 2026, it felt so right that her own name took over. She had been christened SEAL at her launch—and that legacy called for a change of design direction.'
approach: 'I kept the north–south idea, but a new animal made herself present. The polar bear made way for an Arctic seal, weaving the boat’s own name—and her design model, Zeehond (Dutch for seal)—directly into the concept, while the maned wolf stayed for the south. The name SEAL was hers, yet it needed an edge, a colour, to make it truly distinct. Adding “Red” gave it that spark, drawn from the wolf side of my myth: the guará and urucum, the red pigment from Brazil. Red Seal she became. In May 2026, I redesigned the crest: a wolf profile with a seal head cut out of it, above three waves. I tested it at the same level of detail as the bear-and-wolf version, and the new mark held. I built the palette around wine red and sun yellow, and simulated the crest and lettering on the hull and genoa. Finally, I rewrote the myth around the new pair and recorded it in both English and Portuguese.'
outcome: 'The crest is now in use on burgees, stickers, beanies, the boat’s document binders, and the original Van de Stadt design plate, which I had engraved. The bow crest and hull lettering are planned for the April 2027 haul-out.'
