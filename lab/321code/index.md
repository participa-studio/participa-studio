challenge: In 2013, coding was starting to be called a new literacy, but there were few playful ways for young children to learn it. The question for the weekend was whether an app could teach coding in a fun way at each age, and whether parents would pay for it.
approach: 'Startup Weekend gives a team 54 hours to go from a pitched idea to a validated concept. Of the ideas pitched on the Friday, I chose to join the one aimed at young children''s learning, building on what I had learned with LEGO three years earlier. We defined parents as the paying audience, with tech-savvy parents as the early adopters, and native apps for phones and tablets as the channel. I worked on the concept with the team, designed the interaction across four age sets (1–6, 7–12, 13–18 and 19–24), and shaped the story for the final pitch. The marketing plan moved in three steps: awareness, credibility and trial.'
outcome: 'We pitched 321CODE on Sunday, 7 July 2013, and got good feedback from the jury and the audience. The team planned to meet again, but the project stopped after the weekend. For me, it was an interesting experiment: a first hands-on look at designing for learning, years before Participa Studio.'
