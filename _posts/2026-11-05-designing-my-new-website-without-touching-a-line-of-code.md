<p>Over three days, I built my two sister sites, participa.design and participa.studio, together with Claude. I did not write a line of code and barely touched the keyboard. Most of it happened by voice, as a conversation. I am enthusiastic about the result, and the pace was energising. But it left me with a question I cannot avoid: when making becomes a conversation, what is our role as experience designers?</p>
<p>My role did not disappear. It changed. I gave the direction and the tone, reacted to each render and made every call. Claude built, tested and published.</p>
<p>The best moments came from the dialogue itself. After turning down several yellows, I took a photo of a book on my desk, and we used the colour of its cover. I wanted something more playful that recalled movement in nature. We started with a few moving dots and, round after round, they became a swarm of small drawn ants wandering around the Studio home page. None of this was in a brief. It came from the back-and-forth.</p>
<p>It was not effortless. Going faster means more decisions, and each one still needed a designer's eye.</p>
<p>So, my answer for now: when making becomes this fluid, the designer's work is judgement. Knowing what is worth asking for, and recognising the right answer when it appears.</p>
