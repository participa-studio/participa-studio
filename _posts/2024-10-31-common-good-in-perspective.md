<p>Social entrepreneurs have to answer to the parties that fund them, in a language those funders know: the language of business. A social enterprise also connects with far more than a market, including sister NGOs, providers and the communities it serves. I wanted one tool that speaks that business language and keeps all of those connections in view.</p>
<p>The standard business model canvas, <a href="https://www.strategyzer.com/library/the-business-model-canvas">Strategyzer’s</a>, is built around a transaction: someone buys, someone sells. So I built on two adaptations: the <a href="https://socialbusinessmodelcanvas.swarthmore.edu/">Social Business Model Canvas</a> from the Social Innovation Lab at Swarthmore College, which I simplified to help the dynamics of a session, and the <a href="https://www.fullcirclecompetition.com/se">SE Canvas</a> by Full Circle Competition, from which I took Externalities. The main changes: beneficiaries and customers as separate segments; a value proposition split into social and regenerative value; externalities, positive, negative and PESTEL; and surplus and funding next to financial sustainability.</p>
<p>The canvas makes room for diverse stakeholders, from community elders and more-than-human entities to government representatives and business partners.</p>
<p>Next, I will test it with two organisations in Brazil: one in Ubatuba, on the southeast coast, and one in Alter do Chão, in the Amazon region. I want to see how it works in such different contexts, and I would like to hear from others who use similar tools in community settings.</p>
<p>It is not a sales plan. It is a tool to reflect with: to see who the work serves, what it changes, and how it can sustain itself.</p>
<p>I published it as a free template on <a href="https://miro.com/miroverse/social-enterprise-canvas">Miroverse</a> under CC BY-SA 4.0.</p>
<p><em>Update, November 2026: first published on <a href="https://lnkd.in/p/eqYmB4gC">LinkedIn</a> on 31 October 2024. The tests took place in 2024 and 2025, one to one with both organisations. The tool was recognisable, the fields held up, and the entrepreneurs used it to reflect on their own businesses. The template has since been used 52 times, and in September 2026 I used it myself to draft the first concept and business plan for <a href="/lab/refloat/">ReFloat</a>.</em></p>
